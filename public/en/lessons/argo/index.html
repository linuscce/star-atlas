How to find it

Continue south from Sirius ~36° — Canopus, the second brightest star in the sky (−0.7m). Around it are three parts of the ancient huge constellation Argo Navis: Carina, the Keel (with Canopus), Puppis, the Stern (southeast of Canis Major), and Vela, the Sails. Between them and Hydra are the small Pyxis and Antlia.

What it looks like

The Milky Way runs through the Ship. Bright ones: Canopus, Naos, Suhail, γ Velorum, and near Carina the “False Cross” (ε, ι Carinae and δ, κ Velorum), which is mistaken for the Southern Cross.

Good to know

  • Argo Navis was so big that in the 18th century Lacaille split it into Carina, Puppis and Vela. The letters stayed shared: Puppis and Vela have no α or β.
  • Canopus cannot be seen from mid-northern latitudes: it rises only south of latitude 37° N.
  • Spacecraft use Canopus to orient themselves (star trackers).
  • η Carinae is one of the most massive stars in the Galaxy. In 1843 it flared up and briefly became the second brightest star in the sky.
  • Pyxis and Antlia were introduced by Lacaille — he liked to name constellations after instruments.
