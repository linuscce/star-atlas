Cancer constellation — how to find it in the sky

Cancer · Crab, gen. Cancri · Cnc

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

West of (to the right of) the Sickle of Leo, halfway to Gemini, is the faintest constellation of the zodiac. It is easy to find by a hazy spot — the Beehive Cluster (Praesepe, M44), visible to the naked eye outside the city.

What it looks like

An upside-down letter Y of 4m stars; the Beehive is in the center, between the two “donkeys” (γ and δ).

Main stars

  • 3.5mTarf β Cancri
  • 4.3mAcubens α Cancri
  • 4.7mAsellus Borealis γ Cancri
  • 3.9mAsellus Australis δ Cancri

Interesting objects

  • Beehive Cluster, Praesepe, M44 (open cluster)
  • M67 (open cluster)

Good to know

  • Not a single star brighter than 3.5m — the faintest constellation of the zodiac.
  • The Sun is in Cancer from about July 20 to August 10.
  • The Tropic of Cancer got its name because 2000 years ago the Sun was in Cancer at the summer solstice. Because of precession, on that day the Sun is now at the border of Taurus and Gemini.
  • Asellus Borealis and Asellus Australis (γ and δ), the northern and southern donkeys on either side of the Beehive, are “donkeys at the manger”.
