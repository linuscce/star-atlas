Camelopardalis and Lynx — how to find them in the sky

Camelopardalis · Giraffe, gen. Camelopardalis · Cam
Lynx · Lynx, gen. Lyncis · Lyn

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

Two large but very dim “fillers” of the northern sky. Camelopardalis, the Giraffe, is a huge empty area between Polaris, Cassiopeia and Capella. Lynx is a long chain of faint stars from Capella to the front paws of Ursa Major.

What it looks like

There are no bright figures. Remember: the Giraffe is between Polaris and Capella, the Lynx between Capella and the Dipper.

Main stars

  • 4.0mβ Camelopardalis
  • 3.1mα Lyncis

Interesting objects

  • Kemble’s Cascade (a chain of stars)
  • NGC2403 Galaxy NGC 2403

Good to know

  • Legend says Hevelius named it Lynx because you need the eyes of a lynx to find it.
  • Camelopardalis was introduced by Plancius (1612).
  • Kemble’s Cascade in Camelopardalis is a chain of ~20 stars, beautiful in binoculars.
  • At mid-northern latitudes both constellations almost never set.
