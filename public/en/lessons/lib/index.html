Libra constellation — how to find it in the sky

Libra · Scales, gen. Librae · Lib

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

Exactly between Spica and Antares, almost on the same line (the ecliptic), is the dim Libra: a quadrilateral of 2.6–3.3m stars.

What it looks like

The two pans of the scales are Zubenelgenubi (α, “the southern claw”) and Zubeneschamali (β, “the northern claw”). The names recall that these stars were once the claws of the Scorpion.

Main stars

  • 2.8mZubenelgenubi α² Librae
  • 2.6mZubeneschamali β Librae
  • 3.3mσ Librae
  • 3.9mγ Librae

Good to know

  • Libra is the only zodiac constellation that shows an object rather than a living being.
  • 2000 years ago the autumnal equinox point was here (day equals night — “the scales”). Now it is in Virgo.
  • The Sun is in Libra from about October 31 to November 23.
