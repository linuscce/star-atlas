Delphinus, Sagitta, Vulpecula, Scutum — how to find them in the sky

Delphinus · Dolphin, gen. Delphini · Del
Sagitta · Arrow, gen. Sagittae · Sge
Vulpecula · Little Fox, gen. Vulpeculae · Vul
Scutum · Shield, gen. Scuti · Sct

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

Around Altair there are four small constellations. Delphinus is to the northeast (left and up) ~14°: a small diamond with a tail. Sagitta is between Altair and Albireo: a thin arrow. Vulpecula is above Sagitta, almost invisible. Scutum: extend the line Altair → δ Aquilae twice — there is a bright cloud of the Milky Way with the cluster M11.

What it looks like

Delphinus is one of the most lifelike figures in the sky. Sagitta is three or four stars in a line with “fletching”.

Main stars

  • 3.8mSualocin α Delphini
  • 3.6mRotanev β Delphini
  • 3.5mγ Sagittae
  • 4.4mAnser α Vulpeculae
  • 3.8mα Scuti

Interesting objects

  • M27 Dumbbell Dumbbell Nebula
  • M71 (globular cluster)
  • M11 Wild Duck Wild Duck Cluster
  • Coathanger asterism (Collinder 399)

Good to know

  • The stars of Delphinus, Sualocin and Rotanev, are a joke: read them backwards and you get Nicolaus Venator — the Latin name of the astronomer Niccolò Cacciatore.
  • Scutum is named after the coat of arms of the Polish king Jan III Sobieski (Hevelius, 1684).
  • Sagitta is the 3rd smallest constellation.
  • M27, the “Dumbbell” in Vulpecula, was the first planetary nebula discovered (Messier, 1764).
  • On the border of Vulpecula and Sagitta is the “Coathanger” asterism, a fine sight in binoculars.
