Taurus constellation — how to find it in the sky

Taurus · Bull, gen. Tauri · Tau

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

Extend Orion’s Belt to the right and up (northwest) ~20° — the orange Aldebaran, “the eye of the Bull”. It sits in a V-shaped scattering of stars — the Hyades cluster. Farther in the same direction is a tiny “dipper” of 6–7 stars: the Pleiades.

What it looks like

The face of the Bull is the letter V (the Hyades and Aldebaran). Two long horns go east: to Elnath and to ζ Tauri.

Main stars

  • 0.9mAldebaran α Tauri
  • 1.6mElnath β Tauri
  • 3.0mζ Tauri
  • 2.8mAlcyone η Tauri

Interesting objects

  • Pleiades (open cluster)
  • Hyades (open cluster)
  • M1 Crab Crab Nebula

Good to know

  • The Hyades are the nearest open cluster (~150 light-years). Aldebaran (65 light-years) is not part of it — it is just in the foreground.
  • The Pleiades (the “Seven Sisters”) are a cluster of young hot stars ~440 light-years away; 6–7 stars are visible to the naked eye.
  • M1, the Crab Nebula, near the southern horn, is the remnant of the supernova of 1054 recorded by Chinese astronomers; there is a pulsar inside.
  • The Sun is in Taurus from about May 14 to June 21. The Moon often covers Aldebaran and the Pleiades.
  • Elnath (β) used to be counted as a star shared with Auriga (γ Aurigae).
