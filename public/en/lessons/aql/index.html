Aquila constellation — how to find it in the sky

Aquila · Eagle, gen. Aquilae · Aql

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

From Vega to the south (down), ~34° — Altair, the third corner of the Summer Triangle. It is easy to recognize: it has two “guard” stars on either side — Tarazed and Alshain, all three in a line.

What it looks like

Aquila is a diamond or a small cross with a long tail to the south; Altair is on the “neck” between the guards. The Milky Way runs through Aquila.

Main stars

  • 0.8mAltair α Aquilae
  • 2.7mTarazed γ Aquilae
  • 3.7mAlshain β Aquilae
  • 3.4mδ Aquilae
  • 3.4mλ Aquilae

Good to know

  • The Summer Triangle (Vega, Deneb, Altair) is an asterism, not a constellation: its stars belong to three different constellations.
  • Altair is one of the nearest bright stars (17 light-years). It spins so fast (one turn in ~9 hours) that it is noticeably flattened.
  • Altair is almost on the celestial equator (δ = +9°) — it is visible from almost anywhere on Earth.
  • In China and Japan, Altair and Vega are the cowherd and the weaver girl, separated by the Milky Way (the Tanabata festival).
