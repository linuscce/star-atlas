The Milky Way

Open the lesson on the interactive map

In the center of the map is the north galactic pole (in Coma Berenices), so the Milky Way lies as a ring around the edge. It is the disk of our Galaxy, seen from the inside.

Which constellations it passes through

Cassiopeia → Perseus → Auriga → (the edge of Gemini and Orion) → Monoceros → Puppis → Vela → Carina → Crux → Centaurus → Norma → Scorpius → Sagittarius → Scutum → Aquila → Vulpecula → Cygnus → Cepheus → Cassiopeia.

And the other way round: it does not pass through Ursa Major, Leo, Virgo, Pegasus or Pisces — there we look “sideways” out of the disk and see distant galaxies.

Reference points

  • The Galactic Center is in Sagittarius (near the border with Scorpius and Ophiuchus). Here the Milky Way is brightest and widest.
  • The anticenter is in Taurus, near the border with Auriga.
  • The north galactic pole is in Coma Berenices, the south one in Sculptor.
  • The dark lane that “splits” the Milky Way from Cygnus to Sagittarius is the Great Rift: clouds of dust in front of the stars.

By the way: the Milky Way and the ecliptic cross in two places — in Gemini/Taurus and in Sagittarius. That is why the Moon and planets periodically pass in front of the Milky Way exactly there.
