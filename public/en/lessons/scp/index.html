How to find it

The south celestial pole is in Octans; there are no bright stars nearby (σ Octantis is only 5.4m). Between the pole and the Magellanic Clouds is Hydrus. Below the Southern Cross (closer to the pole) is Chamaeleon, next to Carina is Volans, the Flying Fish, and below Triangulum Australe is Apus, the Bird of Paradise.

What it looks like

Everything is faint. Anchors: the Southern Cross, Achernar, the Magellanic Clouds.

Good to know

  • σ Octantis is the “southern pole star”, but it is barely visible to the naked eye.
  • β Hydri (2.8m) is the star brighter than 3m nearest to the south celestial pole.
  • The octant was an instrument that came before the sextant; the constellation was introduced by Lacaille.
  • Volans, Chamaeleon, Apus and Hydrus are constellations of the Dutch navigators Keyser and de Houtman (1590s).
