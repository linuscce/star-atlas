Virgo constellation — how to find it in the sky

Virgo · Maiden, gen. Virginis · Vir

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

Continue the same arc from Arcturus another ~30° to the south — the bright blue-white star Spica. The whole rhyme: “Arc to Arcturus, then speed on to Spica”.

What it looks like

Virgo is large and faint. The center of the figure is Porrima (γ); from it, chains of stars spread out like the letter Y: west toward Leo, north toward Vindemiatrix and southeast toward Spica.

Main stars

  • 1.0mSpica α Virginis
  • 2.7mPorrima γ Virginis
  • 2.8mVindemiatrix ε Virginis
  • 3.6mZavijava β Virginis

Interesting objects

  • M87 Galaxy M87 (first image of a black hole’s shadow)
  • M104 Sombrero Sombrero Galaxy

Good to know

  • Virgo is the 2nd largest constellation (after Hydra).
  • Spica lies almost on the ecliptic: the Moon and planets often pass near it, and the Moon can cover it.
  • The Sun is in Virgo from about September 16 to October 31 — longer than in any other zodiac constellation.
  • The autumnal equinox point is in Virgo.
  • Between Denebola and Vindemiatrix lies the Virgo Cluster of galaxies. At its center is the galaxy M87: in 2019 the first image of the shadow of its black hole was obtained.
