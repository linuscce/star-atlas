Orion constellation — how to find it in the sky

Orion · Hunter, gen. Orionis · Ori

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

The most recognizable constellation in the sky and the second “anchor” after the Dipper. Look for three bright stars in a row — Orion’s Belt. Around them is a quadrilateral: red Betelgeuse and Bellatrix above, blue Rigel and Saiph below. Pointers to the neighboring constellations start from Orion.

What it looks like

An hourglass: the shoulders (Betelgeuse, Bellatrix), the belt (Mintaka, Alnilam, Alnitak), the knees (Saiph, Rigel). Below the belt hangs the “sword” with the Orion Nebula.

Main stars

  • 0.4mBetelgeuse α Orionis
  • 0.2mRigel β Orionis
  • 1.6mBellatrix γ Orionis
  • 2.3mMintaka δ Orionis
  • 1.7mAlnilam ε Orionis
  • 1.7mAlnitak ζ Orionis
  • 2.1mSaiph κ Orionis
  • 3.4mMeissa λ Orionis

Interesting objects

  • M42 Orion Nebula

Good to know

  • The Belt lies almost on the celestial equator (Mintaka has δ ≈ 0°): Mintaka rises almost exactly in the east and sets in the west. Orion is visible from every inhabited place on Earth.
  • Rigel (β) is usually brighter than Betelgeuse (α).
  • Betelgeuse is a red supergiant that will one day explode as a supernova. In 2019–2020 it noticeably faded (the “Great Dimming”).
  • The Orion Nebula (M42) is visible to the naked eye — the nearest large star-forming region (~1350 light-years).
  • The Orionids (~October 21) are meteors from Halley’s Comet.
