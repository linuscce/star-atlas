Hercules constellation — how to find it in the sky

Hercules · Heracles, gen. Herculis · Her

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

Between Alphecca (Corona Borealis) and Vega is a dim quadrilateral of 3m stars, the “Keystone”. It lies almost exactly on the line Alphecca — Vega, closer to Alphecca. This is the body of Hercules.

What it looks like

The Keystone and four “limbs”. Hercules hangs upside down: his head, Rasalgethi (α), is in the south, next to the head of Ophiuchus.

Main stars

  • 2.8mζ Herculis
  • 3.5mη Herculis
  • 3.9mε Herculis
  • 3.2mπ Herculis
  • 2.8mRasalgethi α¹ Herculis
  • 2.8mKornephoros β Herculis

Interesting objects

  • M13 Great Globular Cluster in Hercules
  • M92 (globular cluster)

Good to know

  • M13 is the brightest globular cluster of the northern sky, on the western side of the Keystone. In binoculars it is a fuzzy spot. In 1974 the Arecibo radio message was sent toward it.
  • The Sun and the whole Solar System are moving toward Hercules (the solar apex is on the border with Lyra, not far from Vega).
  • Hercules is the 5th largest constellation.
  • Rasalgethi is a red giant, a variable star.
