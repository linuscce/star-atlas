Cassiopeia constellation — how to find it in the sky

Cassiopeia · Queen, gen. Cassiopeiae · Cas

Open the lesson on the interactive map

How to find it

From Megrez (where the handle of the Dipper joins the bowl) draw a line through Polaris and on for the same distance. There you find the letter W made of five bright stars: Cassiopeia.

What it looks like

Five stars of 2–3m: Caph (β), Schedar (α), γ, Ruchbah (δ), Segin (ε) — the letter W or M, depending on how it is turned. Cassiopeia and the Dipper are always on opposite sides of Polaris: when the Dipper is high, Cassiopeia is low, and vice versa.

Main stars

  • 2.3mCaph β Cassiopeiae
  • 2.2mSchedar α Cassiopeiae
  • 2.2mγ Cassiopeiae
  • 2.7mRuchbah δ Cassiopeiae
  • 3.3mSegin ε Cassiopeiae

Interesting objects

  • M103 (open cluster)
  • M52 (open cluster)

Good to know

  • Like the Dipper, Cassiopeia never sets at mid-northern latitudes.
  • γ Cassiopeiae is an irregular variable: sometimes it becomes brighter than Schedar.
  • In 1572 a supernova flared up here; Tycho Brahe observed it.
  • The Milky Way runs through Cassiopeia.
  • Myth: Queen Cassiopeia is the wife of Cepheus and the mother of Andromeda. The whole family (Cepheus, Andromeda, Perseus, Pegasus, Cetus) are neighbors in the sky.
